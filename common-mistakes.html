Common Mistakes New Students Make

Most of the extra time and money people spend getting a PPL comes from the same handful of mistakes. I made some of these myself. Here's what to watch out for.

1. Flying too infrequently

Flying once every week or two means you spend the start of every lesson relearning what you did last time. Flying 2 to 3 times a week is the single best way to keep hours down.

2. Waiting on the medical

Some students fly for months before getting their FAA medical and then run into a problem that delays or stops their training. Get the medical done before you put serious money into lessons.

3. Putting off the knowledge test

Ground school is easy to push off when flying is the fun part. But the knowledge test has to be passed before the checkride, and leaving it to the end can stall everything right when you're ready.

4. Showing up unprepared

Coming to a lesson without reviewing the maneuvers means paying for the airplane and the instructor while you learn things you could've read at home for free. Read ahead and chair fly before each lesson.

5. Not budgeting for more than 40 hours

Planning around the FAA minimum sets you up to run out of money halfway through. Budget for 60 to 75 hours and treat finishing early as a bonus.

6. Sticking with a bad fit

If your instructor keeps cancelling or you're not learning from them, it's okay to switch. A good instructor relationship makes a huge difference.

For more on why students quit and how to avoid it, check out AOPA's student pilot resources.
