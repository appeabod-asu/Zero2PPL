Glossary of Aviation Terms

Aviation has a lot of jargon and acronyms, and it can feel like a different language at first. Here are the terms you'll hear most during training, explained in plain English. For the full official list, see the FAA Pilot/Controller Glossary.

ATC (Air Traffic Control)
The controllers who keep airplanes separated and give pilots instructions on the ground and in the air.
ATIS
A recorded broadcast at an airport with the current weather, runway in use, and other info pilots need before talking to the tower.
CFI (Certified Flight Instructor)
The instructor who teaches you to fly and signs your logbook endorsements.
Checkride
The final practical exam for your license, given by an FAA examiner. It includes an oral exam and a flight.
Cross-Country
A flight to an airport more than 50 nautical miles away from where you departed.
DPE (Designated Pilot Examiner)
The person approved by the FAA to give your checkride.
Endorsement
A signed entry in your logbook from your instructor that allows you to do things like solo or take the checkride.
Go-Around
Aborting a landing and climbing back up to try again. It's a normal, safe decision, not a failure.
Hobbs Time
The time on the airplane's meter, usually used to bill you for the rental.
METAR
A coded weather report for an airport, updated about every hour.
Sectional Chart
The map pilots use for visual navigation, showing terrain, airports, and airspace.
Squawk
The four-digit code ATC assigns you to set in your transponder so they can identify you on radar.
Traffic Pattern
The standard rectangular path airplanes fly around an airport for takeoffs and landings.
VFR (Visual Flight Rules)
Flying by looking outside the airplane, which is what a PPL allows you to do in good weather.
IFR (Instrument Flight Rules)
Flying by reference to instruments in clouds or low visibility. Requires a separate instrument rating.
